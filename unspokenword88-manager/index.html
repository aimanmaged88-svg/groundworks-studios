Talking to camera

  • Talk to one person. Imagine the exact parent who needs this. Warmer than "hey guys".
  • Lead with the hook — cut the intro. No "so today I wanted to…". First words = the Hook line.
  • Eyes at the lens. Prop the phone at eye height and look at the little camera dot, not yourself.
  • Glance, don't read. Use the teleprompter for the shape; let the words be yours. A tiny stumble reads as honest.
  • One idea per clip. If you have two tips, that's two reels.

Look & sound

Light
Face a window. Light in front of you, never behind. Daytime is free studio light.
Sound
Quiet, soft room (beds, curtains kill echo). Phone within ~50cm, or a cheap clip-on mic.
Frame
Vertical. Your eyes in the top third. Lean in slightly — presence beats distance.
Steady
Lean the phone on books/a mug. Use the self-timer or a cheap remote so you're not reaching.

The teleprompter trick

  • Open a script → tap ▶ Teleprompter. The text auto-scrolls past the teal line.
  • Prop this phone right beside your filming camera, line near the lens — your eyes stay close to the lens.
  • Set the speed to match your natural pace with − / +. Hit pause anytime.
  • Short on hands? Film on one phone, run the prompter on another — or just read it twice, then film from memory.

Editing (free)

  • CapCut (free) — trim, cut dead air, add music. The one app to learn.
  • Burn in captions — always. Most people watch on mute, and it's an accessibility win for your audience. CapCut → Auto captions → check spelling.
  • Keep tutorials under ~45s. Cut every pause and "um". Tight = re-watchable = the algorithm notices.
  • B-roll the food. Film the plate, the pour, the lunchbox while you talk — lay it over your voice so it's not just a talking head.
  • Trending audio, low. Add a popular sound at ~15% volume under your voice for a small reach bump.

The growth loop

  • Hook (0–2s) → stop the scroll with a real pain or a bold line.
  • Value (middle) → one clear, usable idea. Make them feel seen and helped.
  • CTA (end) → "Save this for tomorrow's breakfast" / "Send to a parent who needs it." Saves + shares > likes.
  • First hour → reply to every comment. Early engagement tells Instagram to push it.

Make it a series

Foods that regulate — Part 1, 2, 3… Number them. Series make people follow (they want the next one) and bingeable content keeps them on your page.

  • Same opening frame or first word each time — a tiny signature.
  • End with "Part 4 tomorrow 🤎" to pull them back.
  • Pin your best one to the top of your profile.

Dignity & safety rules

  • Kids' faces — your call, set it once. Backs of heads, hands, from behind, or faces in: decide a rule and keep to it. It's their identity on a public page.
  • You're sharing, not prescribing. No "cure", no "you must". "Here's what helped us" is powerful and honest.
  • In DMs, don't diagnose or give medical advice. "I'm not a professional, but here's what we tried — worth asking your GP/dietitian."
  • Protect your own energy. You can turn off comments on a heavy post. You don't owe anyone the hard days.
